Who Were the Signers

Famous Signers

The Declaration of Independence had 56 signers, and a handful stand out:

John Hancock (Massachusetts)
President of the Continental Congress, whose large, bold signature is the most recognizable. His name is now a common slang term for a signature.
Thomas Jefferson (Virginia)
Principal author of the Declaration. He later became the third U.S. president.
Benjamin Franklin (Pennsylvania)
The oldest signer at 70. A scientist, inventor, diplomat, and writer, he helped edit Jefferson's draft.
John Adams (Massachusetts)
A leading advocate for independence in Congress who served on the drafting committee. He became the second president.
Samuel Adams (Massachusetts)
A key organizer of colonial resistance, involved in the Sons of Liberty and the events leading up to the Revolution.
Benjamin Rush (Pennsylvania)
A prominent physician and social reformer.
Charles Carroll of Carrollton (Maryland)
The wealthiest signer and the last one to die, in 1832.
Robert Morris (Pennsylvania)
A wealthy merchant who later helped finance the Revolution.
Roger Sherman (Connecticut)
The only person to sign all four founding documents: the Continental Association, the Declaration, the Articles of Confederation, and the Constitution.
John Witherspoon (New Jersey)
A minister and president of the College of New Jersey (now Princeton), and the only active clergyman to sign.
